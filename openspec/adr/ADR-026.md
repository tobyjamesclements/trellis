# ADR-026: Consumer realm and identity modes; soft organisations federate with the consumer realm, strict organisations own their identities

## Status
Accepted

## Context
Trellis must serve consumers (individuals) and businesses. Business identity
must be either **soft**, where consumers group together using their own
identities, or **strict**, where the business onboards and creates each user
from scratch. Principals in the first draft were tenant-scoped; there was no
place for an identity that outlives any tenant.

## Decision
- One **consumer realm** per residency zone, operated by Trellis, is a
  tenant with `identity_mode = consumer`. It holds personal identities
  (magic link, social or institutional OIDC), personal profiles, personal
  learning (open courses, personal groups) and the personal credential
  backpack. It is also an OpenID Provider (authorization code with PKCE,
  `id_token`s signed with zone keys) for soft organisations.
- A **soft organisation** is a tenant with `identity_mode = soft`. Members
  authenticate through the consumer realm; the organisation's principal
  for a member is the deterministic id `usr_` + hash(realm issuer, consumer
  sub) (IDE-01) linked by `identity.v1`. The organisation manages
  membership (`enrol.v1`, `role.v1`) and its own courses, cohorts and
  views; it cannot create, reset, suspend or read a member's identity. A
  member can leave, taking their identity and credentials with them; their
  learning facts stay with the organisation and are exportable to them.
- A **strict organisation** is a tenant with `identity_mode = strict`. The
  organisation creates every identity: admin console, SCIM 2.0, OneRoster,
  or its own IdP. Identities are scoped to the tenant, cannot sign in
  anywhere else, and follow the organisation's lifecycle (suspend, delete,
  erase). Credentials are still issued to the person and can be transferred
  to a consumer-realm backpack the person controls, if the organisation's
  policy allows portability (default: allowed at deprovisioning).
- Guests: a strict organisation may admit consumer-realm identities as
  external guests with limited roles under an explicit policy (default off).
- Soft organisations are self-service and cheap: wildcard hosting, no
  per-tenant DNS, certificate or metrics; strict organisations are
  contracted and may have custom hostnames and SSO.

## Consequences
- The consumer realm is the only tenant that acts as an OpenID Provider;
  the machinery already exists for LTI platform launches.
- Data residency: a consumer identity and the organisations it joins live in
  one zone; joining an organisation in another zone means a second identity.
- The registry gains `identity_mode` and `tier`; the admin console gains
  self-service organisation creation for consumers.
- Interop: SCIM 2.0 joins the strict-mode provisioning surfaces (DIO-20).

## Alternatives rejected
- One global identity for everything: needs global uniqueness and breaks
  the strict business model where the business owns the account.
- Modelling soft organisations as cohorts inside the consumer realm only:
  denies them their own courses, views, integrations and admin plane.
