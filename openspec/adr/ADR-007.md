# ADR-007: Retraction by superseding void facts; erasure by cryptographic key destruction

## Status
Accepted

## Context
A grow-only set has no delete. Tombstones in an eventually consistent
system need garbage collection with causal stability guarantees that
Global Tables do not provide. Legal erasure (GDPR Article 17) still applies.

## Decision
- Retraction of meaning is a `void.v1` fact; a fact is effective iff no
  effective void targets it; voids are voidable (FLS-07).
- Personal-data bodies are encrypted under per-subject data keys (ADR-022).
  Erasure deletes every data key for the subject (a terminal write, ADR-003
  class 3), deletes blobs, recomputes views without the subject, and sweeps
  again after the replication window (ADM-11). Backups retain ciphertext
  only.
- Single-fact takedown (legal or moderation) overwrites the body with a
  shred marker in every region (ADM-12); metadata and `fact_id` remain so
  vectors and Merkle summaries are unaffected.

## Consequences
- Plaintext metadata (pseudonymous ids, item ids, timestamps) survives
  erasure; this is documented as the residual and judged acceptable because
  the mapping from id to person is itself erased.
- Every reader of a body must handle "undecryptable" and "shredded".
- Retention is per subject (key granularity), not per course.

## Alternatives rejected
- Physical deletion of facts: leaves copies in PITR backups for 35 days and
  races replication (resurrection); used only as a supplementary sweep.
- Tombstone items with TTL: causal-stability problem; a late replicated
  insert after the tombstone expires resurrects the fact.
